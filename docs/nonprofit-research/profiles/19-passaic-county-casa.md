## Fit assessment

- **Evidence for fit:** Volunteer recruitment depends on prospects understanding the requirements (age 21+, citizenship, background checks, 36-hour training, out-of-pocket travel costs) before an Information Session. Content is split across two domains after the rebrand, which creates navigation friction ("Did Child Focus take over CASA?" is literally an FAQ). A concierge limited to public volunteer and donor information fits; casework would be out of scope [V/I].
- **Risks:**
  - Explicit organizational caution about generative AI [V].
  - The client population is court-involved children, so the confidentiality bar is extreme [V].
  - Low public-intake volume [I].
  - 66% government funding with federal-grant uncertainty [V/I].
- **Unknowns:** volume of volunteer inquiries, info-session conversion, and the Union County history [U].
- **Local meeting practicality:** The Wayne office is about 20–25 minutes from Montclair. The ED holds an M.A. from Montclair State, so local ties are plausible, but don't lead with that [V/I].

## Outreach package (draft — do not send)

- **Value angle:** Child Focus needs a steady stream of qualified CASA volunteers, and every prospect has the same questions before an Information Session: requirements, training, time commitment, costs, whether it's safe. A concierge limited strictly to public volunteer and donor information could answer those any evening and send people to the info-session sign-up. It would never touch case information, which respects the organization's stated position on AI and confidentiality.
