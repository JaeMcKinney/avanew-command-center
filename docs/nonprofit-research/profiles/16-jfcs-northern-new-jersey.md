## Fit assessment

- **Evidence for fit:** Many direct-service programs with real intake friction: a mental-health intake call and therapist match, insurance and sliding-fee questions, a pantry by app appointment only, and an emergency-assistance pathway. Counseling runs in five languages. The phone line closes Friday at 3 PM until Monday. Two offices in two counties, plus school-based programs [V]. A new CEO and COO may be open to fresh approaches [I].
- **Risks:** Sensitive population (mental health, Holocaust survivors, crisis). The demo must be strictly informational and steer clearly to official and crisis lines [I]. A faith-based context needs a culturally careful tone [I]. The new leadership team may still be settling priorities [I].
- **Unknowns:** Inquiry volume; whether an after-hours answering service already exists; board appetite for AI [U].
- **Local meeting practicality:** Good. Teaneck is about 25–30 min away and Wayne about 20 min [I].

## Outreach package (draft — do not send)

- **Value angle:** JFCS answers calls until 9 PM four nights a week, but from Friday afternoon to Monday morning a parent looking for a therapist, a senior's adult child, or a family needing the Corner Market has only the website. A concierge built on JFCS's public pages could explain how intake works, what insurance is accepted, how to book a pantry pickup in Plentiful, and where to find emergency resources. It would always hand off to 201-837-9090 or info@jfcsnnj.org.
