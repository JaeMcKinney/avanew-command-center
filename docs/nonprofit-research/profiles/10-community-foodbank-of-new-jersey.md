## Fit assessment

- **Evidence for fit:** The largest public-facing anti-hunger operation in the state. The site gives multiple phone and text lines, multilingual benefits help, three sites with different hours, monthly-changing mobile pantry schedules, and an 800-partner locator. There is a documented inconsistency in published pantry hours. Neighbors' questions span food, SNAP, WIC, Summer EBT and job training, which is a natural concierge use case [V/I].
- **Risks:** Enterprise-scale procurement and IT governance; the right buyer may be several layers below the CEO [I]. The existing SMS keyword service and Find Food tool cover part of the need [V]. A $300/month product is tiny relative to the budget, which could read as "pilot" rather than "solution" [I]. Exposure to government funding [V].
- **Unknowns:** Volumes on the Food Assistance and Resource Access lines; which languages the multilingual line supports; any internal AI or chatbot projects [U].
- **Local meeting practicality:** Good. Hillside is about 25 min away, and the Garfield Unity Pantry about 20 min [I].

## Outreach package (draft — do not send)

- **Value angle:** CFBNJ already invests in helping neighbors find food: the ZIP-code locator, the multilingual line and the FindFood/Comida text service. A concierge built on CFBNJ's public pages could answer the follow-up questions those tools can't, such as which pantry is open Saturday in Egg Harbor, how to book Garfield through PlentifulApp, or how to get SNAP help, in plain conversational English or Spanish, at any hour. Every answer would hand off to the official lines.

>
