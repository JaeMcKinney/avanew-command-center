## 1. Bottom line

- **All 20 organizations pass the $1.5M revenue screen** on their latest IRS Form 990, and all appear to have 10+ staff. That figure is verified from the 990 for 16 of them and inferred from wage totals for 01, 02, 04 and 05.
- Three pass narrowly and belong on a financial watchlist: **Greater Newark Conservancy ($1.81M)**, **Family Promise of Bergen County ($1.94M)** and **Habitat for Humanity of Greater Newark ($2.35M)**.
- **15 score Tier A (22+ of 30) and 5 score Tier B (16–21). None falls to Tier C.** The rubric favors this list because it was pre-selected for community-facing complexity. Treat the tiers as relative order, not as proof of demand.
- **The preliminary compilation had material errors.** Two used the wrong legal entity (JFS MetroWest pulled a Massachusetts organization's 990; Norwescap matched a holding company with no revenue). One organization has been renamed (Passaic County CASA → Child Focus, Inc.). Seven organizations have leadership changes or site-versus-990 conflicts, and five sets of financials were a filing year out of date. Every profile ends with a **Corrections to preliminary compilation** section.
- **Three organizations need special handling before any outreach.** Ironbound has published opposition to AI data centers. Child Focus/CASA has warned its volunteers against using ChatGPT. Newark Community Street Team's data policy conflicts with transcript tracking. Details are in §4.

## 2. Method and rubric

Each organization was scored 0–5 on six components, for a total out of 30. The anchors were fixed before scoring, and the evidence for each score is in that organization's profile.

| Component | 0–5 anchor |
|---|---|
| Financial capacity | Latest 990 revenue: below $1.5M = 0 · $1.5–2.5M = 1 · $2.5–5M = 2 · $5–15M = 3 · $15–50M = 4 · above $50M = 5. **−1** for a material deficit trend or a filing older than TY2022 |
| Engagement need | Direct public intake, multilingual populations, after-hours relevance |
| Program complexity | Number of distinct programs and sites, and eligibility rules |
| Readiness | Identifiable decision maker, 10+ staff, enough public content for a knowledge base, no strong existing chatbot |
| Proximity to Montclair | Estimated drive: 20 min or less = 5 · 21–30 = 4 · 31–45 = 3 · 46–60 = 2 · over 60 = 1 · outside North Jersey = 0 |
| Strategic value | Referral and network potential, coalition role, brand visibility, fit with the Newark community-development narrative |

**Tiers:** A = 22+, B = 16–21, C = below 16 or failed screen. **Label discipline:** the financial and proximity scores rest on verified 990 data and estimated drive times. Engagement, complexity, readiness and strategic value are **analyst inferences** from public websites, not measured demand. No organization has confirmed call volumes or a pain point.

**Judgment calls to be aware of.** The −1 deficit adjustment went to Eva's Village, Greater Newark Conservancy and Habitat Greater Newark, each of which has consecutive or widening deficits. It was **not** applied in these cases:
- United Community Corporation: a single $1.09M deficit year, though net assets fell 46%
- HomeFront: two deficits, but net assets are rising
- CUMAC: small recurring deficits, plus amended returns
- Family Promise of Bergen: two small deficits against healthy reserves
- Catholic Charities: a one-year deficit, but negative net assets for years

If you prefer the stricter reading, UCC and Catholic Charities would each lose 1 point. UCC would then drop to 25 and Catholic Charities to 28, and neither would change tier.

**Data vintage.** The figures come from the most recent 990s ProPublica has published: fiscal years ending between Dec 2024 and Dec 2025. ProPublica's API extract lags its website, so the newer filings were verified on the organization pages. They were spot-checked independently for Ironbound and BVMI, and revenue and expenses matched to the dollar.

## 6. Outreach rules and cadence (from the specification)

- **Channel:** founder-led, one-to-one email from jae@divigner.com and personal LinkedIn only. No cold calls, mass campaigns or automated sending.
- **Day 0:** personalized email with the organization's demo link. **No pricing** in the first message.
- **Day 2–3 business days:** LinkedIn connection note (each profile includes a draft under 280 characters).
- **Day 5–7 business days:** short follow-up email (drafted in each profile).
- **Later:** one final polite follow-up if appropriate.
- **New Community Corporation:** every draft mentions the current NCC work only as "a community engagement initiative." None implies endorsement or reveals project detail. NCC, the Urban League of Essex County and La Casa de Don Pedro stay out of cold outreach as existing relationships.
- **Pricing**, for the meeting stage only: $4,500 implementation plus $300/month, which includes four hours of conversation. Optional add-ons are priced separately, with no invented rates.
- **Analytics:** don't over-read demo transcripts or quote a visitor's questions back to an executive. Don't assume a transcript means a specific person engaged.

Before sending any draft, replace "[demo link]" and confirm the recipient's first name and title on the organization's site that day.
